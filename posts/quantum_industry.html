My opinion on the emergent quantum industry

06/02/2026

⬅️ Back to all posts

A lot of money is required to build, run, and maintain a quantum computer, and we are not even sure which type of device will be best (Superconductors, Ion Traps, photons, neutral atoms, and many more options). Academia alone could not sustain the financial burden to try so many different devices at scale. We require private investors. The problem is that once you build it and burn the money, the computer may or may not be useful. Beyond Shor’s algorithm, there is a lot of uncertainty about the usefulness of a quantum computer. A lot of promising work, but nothing as concrete as Shor’s.

My opinion is that quantum computers are gonna be useful for highly specific problems. With new quantum algorithms to be discovered. I would like to help discover these new algorithms or, at least, understand them once the community has reached them. I think doing so is a lot of fun!

Right now, we should have a high-risk, high-reward mindset. Many quantum companies will fail, with investors disappointed by the lack of profits, but those that hold up and maintain a healthy balance between science and business will prevail and win big (easier said than done, of course).
